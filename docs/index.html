World Models for Physical AI: Interfaces, Experimental Evidence, and Downstream Utility

Learned world models predict how physical environments evolve, yet higher forecast fidelity does not automatically improve physical behavior. Forecasts enter Physical AI systems through distinct interfaces—training supervision, candidate scoring, action generation, evaluation, or runtime correction—and each role requires its own experimental control.

01 · Training vs. Inference

Future supervision aids learning even without deployment generation

Across reviewed objective ablations, removing future supervision lowers policy success even when the deployed policy reads only current observations. By contrast, isolated execution tests of deployment-time future reading remain sparse.

Fast-WAM Base: 91.8% vs. 83.8% · DyPES-VLA & Cosmos Policy
02 · Selection & Evaluation Reversals

Higher offline fit or evaluator correlation can reverse downstream

Test-time selection gains require mixed-outcome candidate headroom first and closed-loop alignment second. Likewise, a simulator correlating better with existing policies can train weaker new learners.

GeoBoN: 77.4% → 82.5% reversal · DexTouch-WM: r = 0.844 vs. lower X-VLA
03 · Windows & Benchmark Forensics

Runtime fixes and aggregate averages hide task-level failures

For suffix-editable controllers, revisions must beat commitment without lowering net completion. Meanwhile, frozen RoboTwin and RoboDojo snapshots expose severe randomization drops and zero-completion progress credit.

RoboTwin: 32/33 mean drop · RoboDojo: 858/3,536 zero-SR progress

03 · Falsifiable Follow-Up Agenda (Table 10)

Four controlled protocols to resolve the open execution-loop splits

Test 01 · Planning (L5)

Candidate availability vs. scoring rule

Arms & controls
Fix reproducible resets, randomized branch order, candidate pool, continuation policy, and budget K; compare (A) uniform, (B) offline proxy, and (C) candidate scorer (+ K=1 reference & rejected outcomes).
Metrics & decisive criterion
All-starts completion diff, mixed-outcome fraction, latency, and predicted-vs.-realized cross-check; failing to beat uniform at acceptable latency counts against the scorer.
Test 02 · Contact Planning (L2, L4, L5)

Predictive dynamics vs. goal geometry

Arms & controls
Fix sensors, a frozen shared encoder & goal metric, search budget, and replanning; independently swap visual vs. vision–tactile dynamics and contact cost terms (retrain missing modalities—no OOD zero-masking).
Metrics & decisive criterion
Rollout error (L2), object response (L4), closed-loop completion (L5), and latency; lower rollout error without higher completion establishes only an L2 fidelity gain.
Test 03 · Action Generation (L5)

Training supervision vs. deployment readout

Arms & controls
Under one dual-mode architecture and fixed data, compare (A) no future supervision, (B) training-only supervision (deployment readout off), and (C) deployment readout of pure future latents (decoupled from text CoT) at matched compute.
Metrics & decisive criterion
Closed-loop completion, wall-clock latency, and throughput; only a net gain of C over B under matched compute and latency establishes independent test-time readout value.
Test 04 · Runtime Control (L5, L6)

Deadline-bound runtime correction

Arms & controls
Fix observations, base chunk, committed prefix, compute budget, editable window, and hard deadline without pausing physics or hardware; compare (A) unassisted, (B) reactive no-cache, and (C) cached predictive suffix correction.
Metrics & decisive criterion
Net completion across all attempts (including late updates), p50/p95 end-to-end latency, and missed-deadline rate; gains below threshold count against runtime predictive access.
