World Models for Physical AI: Learning to Predict, Plan, and Act in the Real World

A world model may predict the next video frame, imagine a training rollout, compare candidate actions, or help correct a robot’s movement. This survey examines when these predictions improve task performance, using controlled comparisons and failure cases from robotics, navigation, and autonomous driving.

Abstract

What the survey covers

World models predict environmental evolution; action-conditioned variants model the consequences of specified actions. These models increasingly support simulation, policy learning, planning, and evaluation in physical AI.

However, differences in representations, action interfaces, and evaluation protocols obscure when better predictions yield better decisions. This survey reviews data foundations, modeling approaches, and applications in robotics, navigation, and autonomous driving. Drawing on experiments and counterexamples, we examine gaps between recursive accuracy and planning utility, evaluator agreement and policy-training outcomes, and predictive information and execution-time correction. We organize evidence across five uses, identify unresolved attribution questions, and discuss requirements for reliable, action-grounded world modeling.

What this survey contributes · §1.6

Three findings from the reviewed experiments

The paper brings together experiments that separate three questions often treated as equivalent: Does the model predict well? Does its generated data train a useful policy? Does its information arrive in time to change an action?

01

Better rollout prediction can still mean worse planning

GNS shows that training noise can improve multi-step rollouts while worsening clean one-step predictions. ContactWorld’s lowest error-growth representation is not its best planning representation. The survey therefore examines prediction errors alongside task outcomes, rather than using one as a substitute for the other.

02

A better evaluator can produce less useful training data

In DexTouch-WM, WM-Mix agrees better with evaluated policy performance, but WM-Robot trains two of three learners better. IWS also finds that the preferred mix of simulated and real data depends on the learner. The survey compares these two uses of generated experience separately.

03

Useful predictions must arrive before actions are committed

FutureRTC studies which action to start from when a generated chunk is ready. TacPAC uses fresh touch and a cached prediction to revise the remaining part of a plan. The survey traces what each method knows, when it knows it, and which actions it can still change.

What the paper adds to these individual results. It compares the actual intervention in each study: changing a training loss, adding generated data, scoring candidate actions, or exposing a cached prediction during execution. Fast-WAM and LaRA-VLA help separate training supervision from information used to generate actions. Beyond Visual Quality and DreamAvoid show why failed selections cannot be diagnosed without knowing whether a successful alternative was available.

The resulting contribution is a set of study-grounded validation decisions and three follow-up experiments: test rejected actions, separate the predictor from the goal score, and vary predictive context independently of feedback timing. These tests are proposals, not new experimental results. The descriptive framework builds on prior surveys.

Analytical framework · §1.2

What does the model predict, and how is that prediction used?

A generated video, a latent rollout, and a contact prediction are not interchangeable. The survey first records what a model outputs, how actions enter it, and which part of the learning or control process uses its output.

Prediction target

Examples: future images in Visual Foresight, latent trajectories in PlaNet, and contact-related representations in ContactWorld.

Action query

Does the model forecast a supplied action, predict without an action input, generate a desired future, or produce actions and futures together?

Prediction consumer

Dreamer trains a policy on imagined experience. PlaNet uses predicted trajectories to choose actions. TacPAC uses a cached prediction while correcting execution.

What changed?What was measured?What is still unresolved?

For example, removing a video-prediction loss while keeping the action-generation path fixed tests the value of training supervision. Changing the encoder, planner, and goal score together tests a whole system; it does not show which component caused the gain. A model that jointly generates a video and actions also need not support asking what would happen under an independently supplied alternative action.

Foundations and scope · §1

Where the methods and supporting resources fit

The map places methods such as PlaNet, Dreamer, and V-JEPA 2 alongside policies, datasets, and evaluation environments. It is a guide to related research, not a leaderboard or a claim that every entry is a world model.

01

Predicting a future is different from producing an action

OpenVLA maps observations and instructions to actions. V-JEPA 2 exposes an action-conditioned predictor used for planning. Both can help a robot act, but they provide different interfaces for querying the consequences of candidate actions.

02

Datasets and benchmarks are supporting resources

Open X-Embodiment supplies robot data. LIBERO, CALVIN, RLBench, and ManiSkill provide benchmarks or simulation resources. Their presence in the map does not make them learned predictors. Some methods appear in more than one direction because they support more than one use.

Data, representations, and predictive models · §2

How video experience becomes useful for robot actions

A video shows what moved, but not necessarily which robot command caused it. The data chapter compares human videos, robot demonstrations, simulated interactions, and generated trajectories by the information they provide.

01

Two routes from video to an action-conditioned model

V-JEPA 2 adapts action-free pretraining with synchronized robot observations and end-effector changes. DreamDojo instead learns latent-action proxies from human video and replaces them with robot actions during post-training. These are different routes from visual experience to executable-action interfaces.

02

A dynamics model may still need a state-estimation system

GNS predicts from supplied particle states. ParticleFormer first infers point-cloud states from stereo observations, then forecasts them for control. Better dynamics prediction does not by itself solve the problem of obtaining the current physical state from sensors.

Learning, planning, and decision making · §3

Is the future prediction used during training or during action generation?

The paper compares three concrete information paths. In one, future prediction is a training task. In another, an action expert reads a predictor’s internal context. In a third, future video and actions are generated together.

01

Fast-WAM Base

The video head helps train the shared backbone, but the deployed action head does not read future-video tokens. Fast-WAM’s video-loss removal keeps the action readout and inference path fixed, so this comparison tests what predictive supervision contributes during training.

02

V-JEPA Policy

The action expert reads internal predictor context updated by future queries. It does not directly read the final predicted future latents. Nor does this action-generation path ask the predictor to evaluate externally supplied candidate robot commands.

03

Fast-WAM Joint and LaRA-VLA

Fast-WAM Joint generates video and actions through joint denoising. LaRA-VLA separately varies training supervision and information retained at inference. Because its visual and reasoning tokens are tested together, those comparisons do not isolate the visual prediction’s contribution.

Evaluation and synthesis · §4.1

The same model can rank differently on prediction, planning, and training

The following results come from each study’s own protocol. They illustrate why choosing a model requires testing the use you care about, rather than comparing all models with one score.

01

ContactWorld: rollout error and planning success favor different representations

In the 12-task dynamics test, PointCloud+TacRGB has the lowest six-step relative error growth. In the separate 12-task simulated-planning test, PointCloud+TacFF has the highest mean success: 36.1%, versus 32.1% for point clouds alone.

A four-task control study reports 39.5% with full TacFF, 38.6% with a visual-only terminal cost, and 36.5% with matched latent width, versus 32.3% for point clouds. These controls narrow possible explanations, but the learned representation and goal-scoring geometry are still coupled.

Takeaway: A low rollout-error score is not enough to choose the best representation for planning.

02

DexTouch-WM: the better evaluator is not always the better data generator

WM-Mix has higher mean evaluator correlation: 0.844, versus 0.646 for WM-Robot. But policies trained on WM-Robot experience score higher for π0.5 (0.563 versus 0.494) and X-VLA (0.506 versus 0.400). FTP-1 slightly favors WM-Mix (0.694 versus 0.688).

These are mean normalized task scores, not a common binary success rate. Removing X-VLA’s Stand Bottle task reverses that learner’s ordering, so the task mix matters too.

Takeaway: Evaluate generated training data with the actual learner and tasks it is intended to support.

Interpretation boundary. These are within-study results. The normalized task scores, evaluator correlations, planning success rates, and runtime measurements answer different questions and must not be compared as a shared measure of model quality.

Execution reliability · §4.3

FutureRTC and TacPAC change different parts of execution

FutureRTC studies alignment between a generated action chunk and the context at execution. TacPAC studies whether a saved tactile prediction helps correct the unexecuted part of a plan. Their results should not be read as a head-to-head comparison.

01

FutureRTC: start at the action that matches the execution context

With the same execution-time oracle context, starting from the first generated action yields 96.6% success; starting at index 20 after the same delay yields 0.9%. This diagnoses action-index alignment; the reported table does not give the trial or seed count. The oracle uses information unavailable online, so the result is not the performance of a deployable oracle-free system.

02

TacPAC: use fresh touch to revise the remaining actions

Adding access to the cached tactile reference raises mean physical task success from 47% to 64%, with 20 trials per method on each of five tasks. The plan, current tactile input, expert, and parameter count are retained. The result supports the benefit of that reference-access change in this setup; only actions not yet executed are revised.

Diagnostic agenda · §4.4

Three proposed experiments to test what causes the failures

The survey does not stop at identifying a gap. It proposes specific comparisons that could distinguish the remaining explanations. None of the tests below is presented as an experiment already performed by this survey.

A

Was a successful alternative available?

Beyond Visual Quality tests 16 actions at each of 48 restored states. Only six tested states contain both successful and failed continuations. DreamAvoid instead observes executed failures without the physical outcomes of rejected alternatives.

Proposed test: Reset the same state and execute the rejected candidates under a fixed continuation policy. This reveals whether selection missed a successful action in that tested pool.

B

Did the forecast improve, or did the goal score improve?

ContactWorld’s tactile representation influences both future-state prediction and the learned distance to the goal. Existing controls do not fully separate these two contributions.

Proposed test: Fix a compatible encoder, prediction target, and goal metric; vary contact information for prediction separately from contact information used for scoring. Match data, capacity, sensing, and search effort.

C

Did predictive context help, or did fresher feedback help?

Training a policy with prediction losses, anticipating a future observation, and correcting after new touch are different interventions. Comparing their final scores does not isolate one shared “prediction effect.”

Proposed test: Keep the trained policy, sensor stream, and committed actions fixed while varying predictive context or update timing. Report missed action deadlines and task outcomes alongside computation time.

Review scope. This is a structured critical narrative survey with purposive, mechanism-focused, non-exhaustive evidence selection. It does not estimate the field-wide prevalence of benefits or failures, claim a new model architecture, or report the proposed controls as executed experiments.
