\section{Data, Representations, and Predictive Models}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Data: From Observed Motion to Executed Actions}
Training data determine which relationships a world model can learn directly and which require additional alignment. Passive video provides observations of interaction but does not necessarily contain the robot commands that produced it. Robot trajectories can pair images with commands and proprioception, while simulator rollouts can expose additional state variables. These resources provide different supervision; volume alone does not establish suitability for a particular control interface.

DROID supplies distributed real-robot manipulation demonstrations~\cite{khazatsky2024droid}, while Open X-Embodiment aggregates robot datasets and studies cross-embodiment policy learning~\cite{oxe2024}. They illustrate diversity across real scenes and across robot data sources. Neither resource is itself a trained world model. Their relevance is the opportunity to learn relationships among observations, actions, and outcomes, subject to synchronization, sensing, and action conventions.

PhysInOne illustrates a different route: scale through simulation rather than action collection~\cite{zhou2026physinone}. It contains two million rendered videos from 153,810 dynamic scenes, spanning 71 physical phenomena and asset-disjoint train, validation, and test partitions. The suite exposes geometry, motion, physical properties, and text labels, but contains neither robot command trajectories nor real-world validation. Its video-generation comparisons are also mixed across models and fine-tuning methods. PhysInOne therefore supports studying visual physical prediction under controlled synthetic variation, not the claim that scale alone yields executable or real-world dynamics.

For a predictive-control comparison, we retain the action coordinate frame, command duration, control mode, observation frequency, and embodiment. Cartesian displacements, joint targets, and gripper commands are not interchangeable numerical vectors. A video of a human moving an object does not uniquely identify a command for a particular robot. Action alignment may require robot fine-tuning, inverse dynamics, or an embodiment-specific decoder. \subsection{Visual Prediction and Learned Simulation}
Visual world models expose future observations that can be inspected, scored, or passed to another model. This is useful when an outcome is difficult to specify as a small state vector, but depicting a desired future differs from predicting the consequence of an action. UniPi generates a visual plan from a goal and extracts control actions~\cite{du2023unipi}. UniSim learns interactive visual responses to commands and uses simulated experience for downstream learning~\cite{yang2024unisim}. One uses a future as an intermediate plan; the other uses a predictive environment as a source of interaction.

The distinction matters when an agent queries a model repeatedly. A coherent sequence can still misrepresent contact, a grasp, or the displacement induced by a command. We therefore separate conditioning signals from generated outputs. Text-conditioned motion, inferred latent actions, and actuator commands offer different control over a rollout. A single generated clip also differs from an interactive simulator whose next input depends on its own previous outputs.

Learned simulation connects data generation with policy improvement, but creates a validation dependency. A policy can exploit regularities of the learned environment. The relevant test is not only sample plausibility but whether a policy or evaluator trained with synthetic experience performs appropriately in an independently assessed environment. We treat generated experience as an additional data source whose utility must be measured, rather than an automatic substitute for physical interaction.

Interactive World Simulator provides a recent test of both uses~\cite{wang2026interactive}. Its action-conditioned latent dynamics support long autoregressive interaction, synthetic demonstration collection, and policy evaluation. On the reported tasks, some imitation policies trained on 100 generated demonstrations approach counterparts trained on 100 real demonstrations, while $\pi_{0.5}$ retains a substantial real-data advantage. Policy scores in the learned simulator are positively associated with physical scores over four tasks, but the comparison uses training-distribution initial states and shows a slight optimistic bias. This evidence supports a bounded surrogate role, not unrestricted replacement of physical evaluation.

Dexterous World Models (DWM) makes the conditioning interface visually explicit~\cite{kim2026dwm}. Given renderings of a known static 3D scene and an egocentric hand-mesh trajectory, it generates interaction-induced residual dynamics. A 144-clip benchmark, with three random generations per sample, evaluates synthetic and real static- and dynamic-camera cases. The paper also ranks candidate hand trajectories by text--video or image similarity, but this action-evaluation result is a qualitative figure without physical execution or task-success trials. DWM consequently demonstrates visual consequence comparison for human hand-motion candidates, not closed-loop robot control.

\subsection{Latent and Structured Predictive Representations}
Latent models trade direct visual interpretability for representations shaped by learning objectives. Dreamer learns behavior through imagined latent trajectories~\cite{hafner2020dreamer}; TD-MPC2 uses a decoder-free latent model for trajectory optimization~\cite{hansen2024tdmpc2}. V-JEPA~2 separates action-free representation learning from action-conditioned adaptation for planning~\cite{assran2025vjepa2}. Feng et al. instead couple active probing with structured representation learning to seek compact, task-sufficient latent states~\cite{feng2026tasksufficient}. Across DMControl, Meta-World, RoboSuite, and Franka Kitchen simulations, their single-task and transfer comparisons use five seeds, report improved sample efficiency on most tested tasks with Reacher-Hard as an explicit exception, and evaluate skill, compositional, and unseen-task transfer. The study includes no physical robot experiment, so it supports data--representation co-design in benchmark control rather than embodied deployment. Together, these studies show that ``latent'' identifies a prediction space, not a complete training objective or control algorithm.

Structured models make selected physical entities explicit. Graph Network-based Simulators represent systems as interacting particles and learn their evolution through message passing~\cite{sanchez2020gns}. TesserAct instead generates RGB, depth, and surface-normal sequences, reconstructs a time-varying point cloud, and uses an inverse-dynamics model to recover actions~\cite{zhen2025tesseract}. Its nine-task RLBench study reports higher success than its video baseline on seven tasks, but does not include physical closed-loop control. This distinction matters: a geometrically structured future can aid action decoding without being a forward model queried by candidate robot actions.

Physical Object Understanding with a Physically Controllable World Model (PhyWM) learns a 7-billion-parameter autoregressive distribution over local RGB, optical-flow, camera, and pointer tokens from three million real video clips~\cite{venkatesh2026phywm}. Sparse virtual \emph{pokes} enable conditional motion queries and support object, articulated-part, and support-relation inference. On the reported point-prompted SpelkeBench and DragAMove comparisons, its physical-motion representation improves the selected segmentation metrics over the listed baselines. However, the paper explicitly treats visual flow patches as proxies for unavailable action data; the interventions are neither calibrated forces nor robot commands. The evidence supports structure discovery from predictive distributions, not an executed manipulation policy.

ParticleFormer gives a complementary action-conditioned case. It represents rigid, deformable, flexible, and granular materials as labeled point-cloud particles, predicts their displacement under end-effector motion, and places the learned dynamics inside MPPI~\cite{huang2025particleformer}. The paper evaluates six simulated and three physical tasks, including cloth--granular and rope--granular interaction. Its physical control evidence is limited to three rollout trials, and models are trained per scene; the result therefore supports multi-material prediction and MPC within the tested scenes, not broad cross-scene generalization.

MVISTA-4D instead predicts view-consistent RGB--depth futures from a single view and fuses them into a time-varying 3D scene~\cite{wang2026mvista4d}. It recovers actions by optimizing a trajectory latent to reproduce a generated future and then applying a residual inverse-dynamics model. On RLBench and RoboTwin, cross-view, cross-modality, and trajectory-latent ablations each reduce manipulation success. Six physical tasks provide an additional comparison with TesserAct, but the reported offline inference requires tens of seconds on an H200. MVISTA-4D is consequently evidence for geometry as an action-inference interface, rather than real-time forward planning over candidate actions.

Making geometry explicit does not remove state-estimation and control problems. A deployed system still needs to infer state from sensors, connect commands to boundary conditions, and account for unobserved quantities. TesserAct also identifies a concrete representation limit: its single-view RGB--depth--normal sequence captures only one visible surface.

Multimodal prediction raises a related issue. Vision, proprioception, touch, and force need not answer the same question. A contact event can be weakly visible but important for execution. OmniVTA predicts short-horizon visuotactile evolution, feeds predicted tactile features to a diffusion policy, and applies a 60-Hz controller to deviations between predicted and observed touch~\cite{zheng2026omnivta}. On six groups of xArm7 tasks, the full system exceeds its no-controller variant across the reported object, geometry, and perturbation settings. Its ablations support a role for tactile prediction in this system, while the single platform and sensor configuration limit wider conclusions.

ContactWorld controls the predictive architecture and planner while varying visual and tactile representations~\cite{zhang2026contactworld}. Across 12 simulated tasks, point clouds improve average planning success over the two RGB views, while tactile force fields provide the most consistent, but not universal, tactile gain. Its 900 physical trials span six tasks, two robot platforms, and three tactile systems. The controlled Panda comparison favors point clouds combined with structured force fields; the second platform also contains cases in which adding a tactile array does not improve the corresponding visual model. This supports a conditional conclusion: touch is useful when its representation exposes task-relevant contact that vision leaves ambiguous.

Recent systems use tactile prediction at different interfaces. TouchWorld predicts short-horizon tactile subgoals for a slower goal-conditioned policy and reserves recent tactile feedback for a faster residual controller~\cite{zhou2026touchworld}. Its six-task physical study improves over generalist and tactile-policy baselines in both clean and perturbed settings, but the hierarchy changes planning, prediction, and feedback together; the full gain is not attributable to the predictor alone. DexTouch-WM instead aligns human and robot tactile layouts and retargets human motion into robot action coordinates~\cite{qin2026dextouch}. With robot pretraining fixed at five hours, adding up to 100 hours of human interaction improves held-out robot-domain prediction. Its downstream results are deliberately mixed: policy-evaluation agreement improves on average, whereas synthetic trajectories do not consistently replace real training data across policies.

VTAM adds a two-stage alternative: it first predicts future dual-camera and GelSight latents, then freezes that backbone while training an action expert with proprioceptive state and a deformation-derived virtual-force target~\cite{yuan2026vtam}. Each evaluated model executes 80 physical trials across chip grasping, cucumber peeling, and flat or inclined wiping. The full model reports 90\%, 85\%, and 95\% success on the three reported task groups, while a chip-task ablation gives 9/10 successes for the full system, 1/10 without virtual-force regularization, and 0/10 for vision-only or late tactile fusion. This is direct physical evidence for the complete hierarchical visuotactile design. It does not isolate prediction quality: future video and tactile rollouts are evaluated qualitatively, the ablation has ten trials per variant, and the virtual force is an uncalibrated deformation proxy on one sensor and robot.

We therefore ask what each modality makes observable, which future quantities are predicted, how signals are synchronized, and whether downstream use is prediction, planning, or feedback. WorldArena~2.0 expands evaluation toward visuotactile modalities~\cite{shang2026worldarena2}; the studies above provide model-level evidence while leaving longer compositional contact, sensor transfer, and force calibration open.

\subsection{Generalization Across Scenes and Embodiments}
Generalization should specify what changes between training and deployment. An unfamiliar object, a new goal, a shifted camera, and a different robot body impose different demands. Open X-Embodiment provides a data-centered view of heterogeneous robot learning~\cite{oxe2024}; DreamZero studies video--action modeling and transfer with different embodiments~\cite{ye2026dreamzero}. These directions motivate distinguishing transferable visual structure from the mapping to executable commands.

Our synthesis separates representation robustness, predictive validity, and action-interface compatibility. A system may share an encoder across robots while using separate action heads, or retain a predictor while recalibrating control. Both can be useful forms of transfer. Neither should be described as unrestricted embodiment generalization without recording which data, modules, and parameters changed during adaptation.
