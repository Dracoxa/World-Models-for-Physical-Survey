\section{Evaluation, Reliability, and Research Directions}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Prediction Quality and Decision Utility}
Evaluation should begin with the model's claimed role. A representation learner needs evidence about relevant downstream features; an action-conditioned predictor needs tests of the relationship between commands and outcomes; a planner needs evidence about executed behavior. These are complementary questions, not a single evidence ladder that every dataset, theoretical result, and component must climb.

WorldArena compares perceptual quality with functional uses of embodied world models, including data generation, policy evaluation, and planning~\cite{shang2026worldarena}. WorldArena~2.0 expands toward visuotactile modalities, interactive learning, and multiple simulated and physical platforms~\cite{shang2026worldarena2}. This broadens evaluation beyond generated-video appearance. The resulting protocols provide evidence about tested functions and settings, rather than universal certification of a high-scoring model.

Interactive World Simulator illustrates why the functional role also needs an external check~\cite{wang2026interactive}. Besides reporting video metrics over 192-step rollouts, it trains policies on generated demonstrations and compares policy rankings in the learned and physical environments. The observed positive association is more relevant to policy selection than pixel error alone, but it is not a calibration guarantee: the study covers four correlation tasks, training-distribution initial configurations, and a simulator that is slightly optimistic on three of them.

Policy benchmarks answer a related question. LIBERO studies knowledge transfer in robot learning~\cite{liu2023libero}; CALVIN evaluates language-conditioned long-horizon manipulation~\cite{mees2022calvin}. Both can assess a complete system, but task success alone does not identify the responsible component. Jena et al.~\cite{jena2026evaluation} foreground this distinction. Even within the same named suite, task subsets, demonstrations, initialization, and compute must be aligned before attributing differences to prediction.

\subsection{Uncertainty, Constraints, and Runtime Guardrails}
Safety requires an account of what can go wrong, which quantity is constrained, and how the system responds. These choices lead to different claims. PETS propagates model uncertainty through candidate trajectories~\cite{chua2018pets}; CPO optimizes reward under expected-cost constraints~\cite{achiam2017cpo}; neither mechanism alone establishes that a physical robot will avoid every hazardous event. The uncertainty or cost must be tied to an action rule, a safety definition, and an operating distribution.

Formal certificates are conditional. Berkenkamp et al. combine a statistical dynamics model with Lyapunov verification and prove high-probability stability within an estimated region of attraction~\cite{berkenkamp2017safe}. The result assumes, among other conditions, Lipschitz dynamics and policies, calibrated confidence bounds, a Lyapunov function, and an initial safe policy. Its experiment is a simulated inverted pendulum. Thomas et al. instead penalize trajectories that a short-horizon model predicts will become unsafe~\cite{thomas2021imagining}. Their theorem requires a specified unsafe set, a finite horizon within which irrecoverable states fail, and a calibrated set-valued model; experiments on four MuJoCo tasks report fewer, not zero, violations.

Uncertainty-aware shielding offers a third interface. Yu et al. learn a distributional reachability certificate and a shield policy, reporting comparable return with fewer training violations on four simulated tracking and navigation tasks~\cite{yu2022drc}. The paper is a 2022 preprint and lists physical-robot deployment as future work. SafeDreamer integrates cost-aware planning and Lagrangian updates with a learned world model~\cite{huang2024safedreamer}. Across Safety-Gymnasium simulation tasks it improves the reward--cost tradeoff, but its nearly zero-cost result is specific to the benchmark cost definition and does not certify open-world physical safety.

Recovery separates task progress from corrective behavior. Recovery RL learns task and recovery policies, using prior data to identify situations requiring intervention~\cite{thananjeyan2021recovery}. We include it as a complementary safety mechanism, not relabel it as a generative world model. Control barrier functions can restrict control through a specified safe-set condition~\cite{ames2019cbf}; their conclusions still depend on state estimation, dynamics, control authority, and feasibility.

At the system level, Kim et al. divide foundation-model-enabled robot safety into action, decision, and human-centered dimensions and propose monitoring and intervention layers~\cite{kim2026guardrails}. This is an architectural position paper without an evaluation of the proposed stack. It therefore motivates separate decision and action gates, but does not show that the architecture reduces physical incidents. Across these mechanisms, we distinguish guarantees under stated assumptions, empirical violation reduction, recovery demonstrations, and unvalidated design proposals.

Recent systems instantiate narrower parts of this architecture. Liu et al. use latent future rollouts to request human supervision before predicted manipulation failures~\cite{liu2024runtime}; two physical tasks improve over a matched-intervention baseline, although each task uses one human supervisor and the paper does not report a single physical-trial denominator. RoboGuard translates rules and a semantic world model into temporal-logic constraints for an LLM planner~\cite{ravichandran2026roboguard}. On a physical Jackal, it blocks all tested RoboPAIR attacks across 35 harmful prompts while preserving 10 safe behaviors, but assumes an accurate world model and does not cover all dynamics-dependent risks. FEARL separates a foundation-model controller from a small verifiable safety-action module~\cite{corsi2026fearl}. In 18 physical navigation episodes, shielding changes the collision rate from 27.8\% to zero with the same 61.1\% task success; this evidence remains conditional on calibrated low-dimensional safety sensors and the verified input domain.

Failure monitoring is not yet intervention. Foresight feeds action-conditioned V-JEPA~2-AC latents to a causal detector and calibrates time-varying alarms with functional conformal prediction~\cite{zhang2026foresight}. On teleoperated physical rollouts, its Transformer reports ROC-AUC values of 0.93, 0.87, 0.79, and 0.89 across four policy--embodiment settings, leading three settings but not the SmolVLA setting. The detector is evaluated offline: alarms do not stop the robot, request help, or trigger recovery. Its significance level is selected per benchmark for balanced accuracy, cross-policy transfer is asymmetric, and inference takes 183.64\,ms on an H200, over 99\% of it in the world-model backbone. Moreover, the stated false-alarm guarantee requires exchangeability between calibration and deployment data. Thus, the study supports action-conditioned failure monitoring, not reduced physical incidents under distribution shift.

Selective assistance is a separate decision interface. KnowNo requests help when a conformal prediction set for an LLM planner is not a singleton; in its 50-trial physical tabletop study, it matches a simple set baseline's plan success while reducing stepwise help from 0.72 to 0.58~\cite{ren2023knowno}. CoFineLLM explicitly finetunes a planner to reduce prediction-set size and help rate and reports a hardware out-of-distribution demonstration~\cite{wang2026cofinellm}; the published abstract does not by itself establish perception or low-level control calibration. Neither method is a world model. They expose a useful reporting target: coverage must be paired with how often, how long, and at what task stage a person intervenes. ThriftyDAgger makes this burden explicit through intervention frequency, duration, and context switching~\cite{hoque2022thriftydagger}; its physical evidence is a single cable-routing task with 15 intervention-aided trials, so it is a measurement precedent rather than broad reliability evidence.

\subsection{Operational Reliability and Attribution}
Reliability depends on the complete observation--decision--execution loop. A planner can act on a stale observation; a controller can saturate; an action chunk can continue after the scene changes. This motivates reporting end-to-end latency and action timing, not just network inference time. The Astra evaluations in Section~\ref{sec:policy} also illustrate why simulation timing must be explicit: pausing an environment removes consequences of reasoning delay that remain present during continuous physical operation.

Attribution requires controls at the relevant interface. To assess a predictive planner, hold candidate generation and scoring fixed where possible. To assess predictive pretraining, compare training signals with matched data and compute. For a reasoning-policy hybrid, distinguish additional observations, additional inference, proposal editing, and explicit consequence prediction. Not every study needs every comparison; the comparisons determine which explanation the study supports.

A practical reporting unit is an episode with its outcome, elapsed time, intervention history, and failure type. Aggregation should retain denominators and variation across tasks, seeds, or environments. Reports that reuse experiments should be linked as evidence families when appropriate. This is important for rapidly released systems where an early project report and an expanded preprint may contain overlapping trials.

Recovery also needs a stated endpoint. LIBERO-Recover separates action retry, action adaptation, task-relevant object-state restoration, and environmental restoration across more than 1,000 simulated scenarios~\cite{liu2026liberorecover}. Six evaluated policies perform substantially better on the two action-level categories than on state-level recovery, and mixed recovery training improves recovery-set results without consistently improving standard LIBERO success. Because the benchmark is simulated and starts from constructed failure states, it does not by itself establish online failure recognition or physical recovery. It does provide a useful reporting distinction: avoiding a predicted hazard, correcting one action, restoring the state, and resuming the original task are different outcomes.

\subsection{Research Directions and Synthesis}
\paragraph{From prediction scores to useful decisions.}
An important question is how changes in predictive quality alter decisions made with the model. This requires examining variables used by a planner, not just a visual aggregate score. Functional benchmarks provide one entry point~\cite{shang2026worldarena}. Further comparisons should examine cases in which plausible predictions misrank actions or omit consequential alternatives.

A recent risk-informed world-model perspective sharpens this gap as three mismatches: likelihood versus risk, prediction versus intervention, and finite prediction horizon versus accumulated consequences~\cite{ma2026riskinformed}. Its proposed representation, counterfactual reasoning, memory, and assurance components form a research agenda rather than an evaluated architecture. The testable question is whether preserving rare, action-changing evidence alters action ranking, intervention, recovery, or abstention under matched information and compute.

\paragraph{Contact, hidden state, and embodiment.}
The next literature pass should connect structured and multimodal models to concrete sensing and actuation problems: contact prediction, deformable interaction, touch and force, and robots with different control conventions. Each comparison should distinguish observing a previously hidden variable, predicting it more accurately, and changing how a policy uses it. These are collection priorities, not claims that relevant literature is absent.

\paragraph{Adaptation and computation.}
Online updates, longer memory, and larger inference budgets change a system's operating conditions. Comparisons should report adaptation data, regression, compute, and behavior under delayed or failed inference. Prediction and control need not run at the same rate. The design question is how to allocate computation while maintaining a response when prediction is uncertain, unavailable, or too slow.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
World models connect experience to action through learned simulation, predictive representation, online planning, imagined learning, and joint world--action generation. These routes differ in what is predicted and where predictions are consumed. The review follows those dependencies from data and representation to behavior and reliability. The next stage is to broaden primary evidence for each route, especially contact-rich interaction, navigation and driving, and safety under distribution shift, while preserving the source-specific interpretation of each comparison.
